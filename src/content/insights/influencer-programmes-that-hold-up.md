Indonesia has one of the most active creator economies in the world, and one of the easiest ones to waste money in. The difference between the two usually comes down to decisions made before the first creator is ever contacted.

## Start with mapping, not shopping

The most common mistake in influencer marketing is starting from a list of names. Reach is the last filter to apply, not the first. The questions that actually predict performance:

- Does this creator's audience overlap with the people the brand needs to reach — or just resemble them demographically?
- Has the creator built credibility in this category, or would the endorsement read as a detour?
- Does their content style survive a brand brief, or collapse into an ad read?

Proper mapping answers these before budgets are committed. It is slower than shopping from a rate card. It is also the reason some campaigns produce advocacy and others produce inventory.

## Relationships outperform transactions

A creator posting for a brand the third year running communicates something a one-off post cannot: continuity reads as conviction. This is why our influencer practice is structured around ongoing relations — first approach, relationship management, then campaigns — rather than campaign-by-campaign procurement.

The practical benefits are unglamorous and decisive: better rates, better content, faster turnarounds, and creators who flag problems early because the relationship is worth protecting.

## Measure what the brief asked for

Every influencer campaign should end the way it began — against the brief. Impressions are an output; the brief defined an outcome. Monitoring and evaluation should track sentiment, message pull-through and audience response, not just delivery counts.

More than 40,000 reviewer and influencer posts into this discipline, our conclusion is unchanged: creators are a media channel with feelings. Treat the channel strategically and the people well, and it performs. Reverse the two, and it does not.
