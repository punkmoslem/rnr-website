Every year, someone declares the press release dead. Every year, the brands that hold the strongest reputations in Indonesia keep earning coverage — and the numbers keep proving the point.

## Trust is not transferable

Paid media buys attention. Earned media borrows credibility — the journalist's, the publication's, the editor's judgement that this story deserves space. That distinction matters more in Indonesia than in many markets, because Indonesian audiences remain highly attuned to the difference between advertising and news, and because national media still shapes the agenda for regional outlets and social conversation alike.

When a health desk covers a public education campaign, or a technology editor benchmarks a product launch, the coverage carries a signal no ad budget can replicate: *someone independent decided this mattered.*

## The compounding effect

A single placement is a moment. A sustained media programme is an asset. Across our longest retainers — several running for seven years or more — the pattern repeats: coverage compounds. Journalists who covered the first campaign answer the second call faster. Spokespeople who performed well get invited back. The brand becomes a reference point rather than a pitch.

This is why we counsel clients to treat media relations as a programme, not a series of announcements. The 759 articles behind one recent healthcare campaign were not the product of one big moment — they were twelve deliberately sequenced activities, each building on the last.

## What earned media demands in return

Earned coverage has a price, and it is not paid in rupiah. It is paid in:

- **A real story.** Editors are not obliged to care. The angle has to survive contact with a newsroom.
- **Genuine access.** Spokespeople who show up, answer directly, and respect journalists' time.
- **Consistency.** One available quote in a crisis is worth ten press conferences in calm weather — but only if the relationship existed before the crisis.

Brands that pay that price hold reputations that discounting competitors cannot buy. Twenty-six years in, we have yet to see an exception.
